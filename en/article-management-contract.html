Management Contracting: Transparency Beyond a Fee Percentage

Construction

A management-contract model only becomes transparent when procurement, approvals, invoices, subcontractor scope, schedule and changes are traceable. The fee percentage is one line in a much larger control system.

Why this matters

Owners should be able to see committed cost, paid cost, forecast-to-complete, upcoming procurement decisions and the effect of change orders. Good reporting turns management contracting from a trust-based arrangement into a controlled project system.

In real projects, decision quality depends on input quality. Teams should distinguish observations, assumptions, requirements and decisions—and document each one clearly.

ARNO framework

ARNO turns a general recommendation into a workflow: defined input, named responsibility, control gate, measurable output and a recorded next decision. The same structure can be used by both design and site teams.

Decision checklist

  • Record scope and assumptions before action.

  • Identify the technical reference or drawing used for control.

  • Name the decision owner and deadline.

  • Review cost, time and quality risk before execution.

  • Keep evidence that the decision or corrective action was closed.

Note: This is educational and management content. For regulations, contractual duties or legal matters, verify current project requirements and consult appropriately qualified professionals.
